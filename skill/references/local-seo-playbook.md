# Central Florida local SEO playbook

Order matters: real eligible business, then one canonical identity, then verified map platforms, then a technically sound site, then genuine reviews, then authoritative citations and links, then useful local content, then continuous measurement. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance, and prominence; nobody can pay for local rank, and service areas never cancel out distance.
The first wave of assets, in order: GBP, then the website and domain, Search Console, GA4 with GTM, Bing Places, Apple Business, core citations and aggregators, the review system, Central Florida orgs, and then ongoing content and links. Retailers also set up Merchant Center with local inventory and free listings.
Two failure modes to avoid: optimizing on top of the wrong business identity, and chasing low-value SEO signals instead of real prominence, relevance, and reputation.

## 1. Operating model
- Storefront (customers visit, staffed): show the real address, permanent signage matching the GBP name, a real entrance, posted hours that match the profile.
- Service-area business (travels to customers, or works from home or no office): verify with the real address but hide it publicly. No fake signage, no public home address, no geo coordinates. Up to 20 real service areas, within about a 2-hour drive, chosen from actual customer economics, not a wish list of keywords.
- Hybrid: show the qualifying staffed address plus service areas.
- Multiple locations only if each is genuinely staffed, each with its own profile and page. Never invent locations or coordinates.

## 2. Canonical business record (freeze before any submission)
Record these: legal entity; public name (the real-world name, no keyword stuffing); address and its visibility; an owner-controlled local phone (not a call center); one HTTPS domain, never alternated in citations; domain email such as hello@; the most specific primary category plus only real secondary ones; hours and a holiday process; a factual description (GBP allows 750 chars, no URLs or HTML); real service areas; lat/long only for a real public location; one official profile per social network. Every move, phone change, rebrand, DBA change, or hours change starts here, then propagates everywhere.
Keep a verification evidence folder: sign, street, and interior photos (storefront); lease or deed; utility bill; licenses; business-tax docs; insurance; branded vehicle and equipment photos (key for service-area businesses); a matching invoice; DNS access; phone account access; domain email. Upload only when a platform asks.

## 3. Platforms (owner-controlled master accounts; delegate staff and agencies as users)
- GBP (critical): search for an existing profile first and claim it, never duplicate it. Fill in the name, categories, address or service-area status, phone, URL, hours (normal, special, and category-specific), services and products, attributes, booking links, description, and real photos and video. Get the review link and QR from the Get more reviews section. Put `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp` on the website field. Record the performance baseline before optimizing. Google picks the verification method: phone or SMS, email, postcard, video recording (continuous, showing the location, signage or equipment, and proof you manage it; service-area businesses show vehicles, equipment, and documents), or live. Prepare for video rather than expect a visit.
- Search Console (critical): a Domain property verified by DNS, submit the sitemap, inspect key URLs, monitor indexing.
- GA4 through GTM (critical). Events: generate_lead on a successful form, booking as a key event, click-to-call, email click, SMS or WhatsApp, directions, quote calculator, purchase, appointment completed. Use distinct UTMs for chambers, newsletters, sponsors, and partners.
- High: Bing Places (claim or add), Apple Business (the new name for Apple Business Connect; verify the organization, which takes days, then the place), Data Axle, TransUnion Digital Business Profile (the current Localeze, 80+ outlets). Medium: Foursquare. Retail only: Merchant Center. Don't follow old lists naming Acxiom, Factual, or Neustar Localeze.

## 4. Website
- Search Console tells you queries, impressions, clicks, and indexing. GA4 tells you what visitors do after they land.
- Architecture: about, services with a child page per service, service areas with only materially unique city pages (real jobs, neighborhoods, logistics, unique photos, local testimonials, FAQs, proof), projects or case studies, reviews, resources, and contact. No doorway pages that just swap the city name. Don't repeat the city unnaturally in headings, footers, or alt text.
- Each money page needs: a short stable URL; a title with the service, local context, and brand; an H1 naming the subject; an opening that states the service, the customer, and the area; specific deliverables; proof (photos, projects, credentials, process); natural geography; a mobile CTA with phone, form, or booking; internal links to related service, city, case study, and contact pages; the canonical name, address, and phone; matching hours; original compressed images with meaningful alt text; real FAQs; unique copy; trust signals (team, experience, license, insurance); and a clear conversion path.
- Technical: important pages return 200, aren't accidentally noindexed or blocked in robots.txt, and are in the sitemap. One HTTPS canonical, with HTTP and alternate hosts redirecting. Old URLs redirect to their closest match, not the home page. Inspect URLs after changes. Check mobile nav, forms, tel links, and maps. Compress and lazy-load media, keep third-party scripts light, monitor CWV. No mixed content.
- LocalBusiness schema with the most specific subtype, matching visible facts. Use geo only for a real public place. For a service-area business, leave out the private address and coordinates; use name, URL, phone, and areaServed, consistent with GBP. Don't mark up self-chosen testimonials for stars. Validate after deploy.

## 5. Citations
Authority, relevance, and accuracy over count. Tiers: core maps (GBP, Bing, Apple); consumer platforms (Yelp, Facebook and Instagram, BBB); data sources (Data Axle, TransUnion, Foursquare); optional distribution (Yext, Semrush Local, BrightLocal citation building) to speed up the long tail, while you own Google, Apple, Bing, and key vertical profiles directly; then industry sources.
Industry layer: trades (BBB, Angi, Houzz, manufacturer and dealer directories, trade associations); B2B, marketing, and software (Clutch, local tech and business groups); hospitality (TripAdvisor, reservation platforms); healthcare (Healthgrades, insurer networks); legal (Avvo, Justia, FindLaw); weddings (The Knot, WeddingWire, preferred-vendor pages); real estate; automotive; retail (Merchant Center).
Central Florida: join 2 or 3 orgs where the business really participates. Options: Seminole County Chamber, The Osceola Chamber, West Orange Chamber, Winter Park Chamber, Lake Nona Regional Chamber, Hispanic Chamber of Metro Orlando, African American Chamber of Commerce of Central Florida, an Orlando-area chamber, Visit Orlando or Experience Kissimmee (tourism-facing only), and UCF's chamber list for others.
Cleanup sheet columns: platform, URL, name, address, phone, website, category, hours, claimed, login owner, verification status, correct, duplicate, duplicate URL, submitted date, resolved date, notes. Search for the exact name, phone, address plus name, and old names, phones, and addresses. Resolve in this order: GBP (never make a second profile), then Apple and Bing, then Data Axle, TransUnion, and Foursquare, then Yelp and industry sites, then chambers and secondary directories. Mark closed old locations as closed or moved. Any change to the name, address, or phone means a full citation change project, not just Google.

## 6. Reviews
Process for every eligible customer: a neutral request, Google's link or QR, ask for an honest review, at most one reminder, the same flow for everyone (no gating by satisfaction), respond to reviews, and flag policy-violating reviews through the platform, never threaten reviewers.
Request template: "Hi [First Name], thanks for choosing [Business]. Would you be willing to share an honest review of your experience? Your feedback helps us improve and helps other local customers know what to expect. [Google Review Link] Thank you, [Name]". In person: "We send customers a link where they can share an honest Google review. Feedback, good or bad, really helps us improve." Positive reply: thank them, mention a non-sensitive detail. Negative reply: thank them, apologize, invite them to contact a business channel to resolve it. In privacy-sensitive industries, keep replies generic.
Compliance: no fake, insider, or sentiment-conditioned reviews, no fake independent review sites, no suppression (FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule). No contract clauses that restrict honest reviews (Consumer Review Fairness Act). Never offer cash, discounts, gifts, contest entries, or freebies for a Google review. Never solicit reviews on Yelp. Check regulated verticals before automating.
Review KPIs: total, rating, new per month, % responded, median response time, requests sent, request conversion rate, positive and negative themes, platform mix. Don't chase a perfect 5.0.

## 7. Local content and links
Content only a business really operating locally could make: real case studies (problem, neighborhood, solution, photos, timeline, result), service plus local-market expertise, local FAQs (pricing drivers, service boundaries, permits, seasons, logistics), partnerships, events, original photos. About one excellent piece a month plus GBP photos and updates. No mass ZIP-code pages.
Links come from real relationships: an active chamber profile and participation, tourism orgs if relevant, sponsorships of local nonprofits and events, vendor, supplier, and partner pages, preferred-vendor lists, local media (data, commentary, stories), associations, useful community resources, joint case studies, hosted workshops. Never buy DA packages, PBNs, or directory bundles.

## 8. Measure
Don't measure rank from one office computer; use geo-grid tracking (Semrush Local, BrightLocal). Layers: visibility (map grid share, impressions, brand vs non-brand, visibility by service and city), engagement (GBP website clicks, calls, directions, CTR), conversion (qualified calls, forms, bookings, lead-to-customer %, revenue by channel), authority (reviews, response %, citation accuracy, unresolved duplicates, local referring domains, index coverage). The real KPI is qualified local revenue.
Monthly dashboard: local organic qualified leads, GBP qualified actions, lead-to-customer rate, organic and local revenue, local organic clicks, geo-grid pack visibility, brand vs non-brand impressions, new reviews, average rating, median response time, citation accuracy %, new local referring domains, top service pages by conversions, top service areas by qualified leads.

## 9. Roadmap and maintenance
P0: canonical record, operating model, premises and signage (storefronts only), local phone, domain and email, evidence folder, find or claim GBP, configure GBP and service areas, GA4 and GTM, Search Console, sitemap and inspection. P1: Bing, Apple, core pages, unique area pages, schema, technical audit, data sources, industry profiles, Merchant Center (retail), review QR and link, post-sale review workflow. P2: citation and duplicate audit and fixes, chambers, tourism and industry orgs, the first 3 case studies, outreach, geo-grid baseline, KPI dashboard. Expect roughly 35 to 90 hours of hands-on work for one location, plus platform wait times.
Daily: urgent reviews and messages, suspensions or bad edits, and check that phones and forms work. Weekly: answer reviews, add photos, spot-check the profile, check lead tracking. Monthly: KPI exports, geo-grid, GSC issues, citations and duplicates, one proof asset, outreach. Quarterly: full GBP, category, hours, and services audit, citation audit, membership review, technical crawl, schema validation, internal links, competitors. Before holidays: set special hours everywhere. Twice a year: new photos, a service-area profitability review, refresh weak pages, judge memberships by results. Annually: full entity audit (name, address, phone, domain, licenses, memberships, citations, redirects, old locations, analytics access, account ownership). After a move, rebrand, or phone change: update the master record first, then the site and schema, GBP, Apple, Bing, aggregators, citations, chambers, and industry sites, and watch for old data for months.

## Rules
Never invent facts, stats, reviews, or locations. When platforms change, check first-party docs (Google Business Profile help, Search Central, GA4 docs, Bing Places, Apple Business, Merchant Center, FTC and platform review policies, the chambers' own directories). Report each item as done, gap, or needs the owner.
